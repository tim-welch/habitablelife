# 📘 Phase 0 Syllabus: The Gospel Message in the New Testament

_(6-Week Version)_  

---

## 🏷️ Basic Information

**Phase #:** 0  
**Phase Title:** _The Gospel Message in the New Testament_  
**Duration:** 6 weeks  
**NT Books / Passages in Focus:**

- Mark 1:14–15; Matthew 4:17; Luke 4:16–21; 24:44–49
    
- John 3; 20:30–31
    
- Acts 2; 3; 10

- 1 Corinthians 15:1–8

**Greek Focus:**

**Assignments Required:**  
Exegetical notes, Greek word studies, OT background studies, weekly teaching summaries, final phase reflection

**Expected Weekly Time:** ~5 hours/week

---

## 1. 🎯 Phase Purpose

This phase establishes a text-derived, inductive understanding of the gospel as proclaimed by Jesus, the apostles, and the earliest church. It trains you to read these passages in their literary context, observe how the New Testament authors define and explain “gospel,” and see how OT promises shape their message. You will begin using basic tools of Greek study, focusing on how key words carry meaning in context. Throughout these six weeks, you will practice concise teaching skills, short written summaries, and foundational exegetical habits.  

---

## 2. 🧭 Learning Outcomes

By the end of Phase 0 you will be able to:

1. Identify how Jesus, the apostles, and Paul articulate the gospel message.
    
2. Explain the authorial purpose in each core gospel passage (e.g., Mark 1:14–15; Acts 2; Romans 1–5).
    
3. Trace OT background texts behind NT gospel presentations.
    
4. Compare MT and LXX readings where relevant to NT citations.
    
5. Perform basic Greek word studies using lexicons and parsing tools.
    
6. Articulate major gospel themes: Kingdom, Messiah, repentance, faith, forgiveness, resurrection.
    
7. Write concise 1–2 paragraph teaching summaries.
    
8. Produce a short (300–600 word) “gospel synthesis” essay.
    
9. Demonstrate inductive reading skills in notes, observations, and summaries.

## 3. 📅 Phase Schedule Overview (6 Weeks)

---

### Week 1 — Jesus’ Own Gospel Proclamation

**Primary Texts:**  
Mark 1:14–15; Matthew 4:17; Luke 4:16–21

**Greek Focus:**  
_euangelion_, _basileia_, _metanoeō_ — introductions to lexical study

**OT Background:**  
Isaiah 40–61 (esp. Isa 40:9; 52:7; 61:1–2)

**Assignments:**

- Exegetical notes (1–2 pages)
    
- Greek word study: _euangelion_
    
- Teaching summary (1 paragraph): “What Jesus proclaimed as gospel”
    

---

### Week 2 — The Risen Christ’s Commission

**Primary Texts:**  
Luke 24:44–49; John 20:30–31

**Greek Focus:**  
_pistis_, _hamartia_, _aphesis_

**OT Background:**  
Psalm 2; Psalm 110; Isaiah 53

**Assignments:**

- OT connection chart comparing Luke 24 claims with OT passages
    
- Greek word study: _pistis_
    
- Teaching summary: “Repentance and forgiveness in Luke’s gospel theology”
    

---

### Week 3 — The Gospel in the Apostolic Preaching (Part I)

**Primary Texts:**  
Acts 2; Acts 3

**Greek Focus:**  
_kerygma_ dynamics (verbs of proclamation): _martureō_, _kēryssō_, _metanoeō_

**OT Background:**  
Joel 2; Psalm 16; Psalm 110

**Assignments:**

- Exegetical notes on Peter’s sermons
    
- OT quotation comparison (MT vs. LXX)
    
- Short essay (300–400 words): “What Peter proclaims as gospel”
    

---

### Week 4 — The Gospel in the Apostolic Preaching (Part II)

**Primary Texts:**  
Acts 10

**Greek Focus:**  
_sōtēr_, _dikaiosynē_, _eirenē_

**OT Background:**  
Isaiah 52; Genesis 12; covenant blessing themes

**Assignments:**

- Exegetical observations on Peter’s message to Gentiles
    
- Greek word study: _dikaiosynē_
    
- Teaching summary: “The gospel for the nations (Acts 10)”
    

---

### Week 5 — Paul’s Gospel (Part I): Romans 1–3

**Primary Texts:**  
Romans 1–3

**Greek Focus:**  
_gospel_ vocabulary in Paul; _dikaioun_, _hilastērion_ (intro)

**OT Background:**  
Genesis 12 (Abrahamic promise); Habakkuk 2:4; Psalm 14

**Assignments:**

- Exegetical notes on Romans 1–3 argument flow
    
- OT intertext chart (Rom 1–3)
    
- Learning exercise: Trace Paul’s definition of “gospel” from Rom 1:1–5, 16–17
    

---

### Week 6 — Paul’s Gospel (Part II): Romans 4–5 + NT Summary

**Primary Texts:**  
Romans 4–5; 1 Corinthians 15:1–8

**Greek Focus:**  
_logizomai_, _charis_, _katallagē_

**OT Background:**  
Genesis 15; Genesis 17; Adam typology (Genesis 1–3)

**Assignments:**

- Exegetical notes on Romans 4–5
    
- Final gospel synthesis essay (300–600 words)
    
- Phase Reflection (end-of-phase exercise)

## 5. 🇬🇷 Greek Learning Plan

**Vocabulary Goals:**  
+35–50 words across six weeks (major gospel-related terms)

**Grammar / Syntax Goals:**

- Learn to identify verbs, nouns, participles in lexical form
    
- Understand basic present/ aorist distinctions
    
- Use lexicons responsibly (BDAG/Step Bible)
    

**Greek Reading Goals:**

- Recognize and define key gospel words in context
    
- Attempt short phrase translations of Mark 1:14–15 and Acts 2 excerpts
    

**Greek Assignments:**

- 3–4 targeted word studies
    
- Parsing exercises (simple present and aorist verbs)
    
- Short translation attempts (optional)

## 8. 🔗 OT Integration Goals

By the end of this phase you will be able to:

- Trace how NT authors interpret Isaianic new-exodus texts
    
- Understand how Joel 2 frames Pentecost
    
- Connect Abrahamic covenant promises to Paul’s gospel in Romans
    
- Recognize MT–LXX differences in Acts 2 and their interpretive implications
    
- Identify major OT themes: Kingdom, Messiah, Spirit, Covenant, Redemption

Your progress will be evaluated based on:

- Clear inductive observations
    
- Proper handling of OT context and quotation
    
- Clarity and coherence in summaries
    
- Ability to articulate gospel themes without importing later systems
