# 🇬🇷 Greek Helps Sheet — _εὐαγγέλιον_ (_euangelion_)

**Meaning:** good news, glad tidings, gospel

---

## 1. 📚 Lexical Core

### Lexical Form:

**εὐαγγέλιον** (_euangelion_) — noun, neuter, nominative singular

### Related Words (Word Family):

- **εὐαγγελίζω** (_euangelizō_) — to announce good news
    
- **εὐαγγελιστής** (_euangelistēs_) — one who announces good news
    
- **εὐάγγελος** — bearing good news (rare)
    

### Basic Gloss:

“good news,” “gospel,” “announcement of victory or royal news”

### Semantic Range (core NT sense):

1. **Good news of God’s saving action**
    
2. **Announcement of the Kingdom’s arrival**
    
3. **Proclamation concerning Jesus the Messiah**
    
4. **The message preached by Jesus and the apostles**
    

> **Important:** In the NT, _euangelion_ is **not** an abstract concept.  
> It is always **the announcement of something God has done** and is doing.

---

## 2. 📖 Background: Septuagint (LXX) Usage

### Key OT roots behind the term (LXX):

- **Isaiah 40:9** — “Go up… O herald of _good news_ (εὐαγγελιζόμενος).”
    
- **Isaiah 52:7** — “How beautiful… who brings _good news_ of peace.”
    
- **Isaiah 61:1** — “He has anointed me to _proclaim good news_ to the poor.”
    

### OT Meaning:

The “good news” in Isaiah is the **announcement of God returning as King**, delivering His people, restoring His reign, and bringing salvation.

**Jesus directly uses this Isaianic pattern** (particularly Isaiah 61) when He announces the gospel.

---

## 3. 📍 How Each NT Author Uses _euangelion_

### A. Mark

- Mark 1:1 — “The beginning of the _gospel_ of Jesus Christ, the Son of God.”  
    → Mark frames his whole book as “the good news.”
    
- Mark 1:14–15 — Jesus proclaims the gospel:
    
    > “The time is fulfilled, the kingdom of God has come near. Repent and believe the gospel.”
    

**Meaning in Mark:**  
The “good news” = **God’s reign arriving through Jesus**.

---

### B. Matthew

Matthew rarely uses the noun but uses the _verb_ (_euangelizō_) conceptually:

- Matthew 4:17 parallels Mark 1:15
    
- Matthew 11:5 — the poor have the good news preached to them (Isaiah 61 echo)
    

**Meaning in Matthew:**  
The “good news” = **fulfillment of the Scriptures in Jesus the Messiah**.

---

### C. Luke

- Luke 4:18 — Jesus reads Isaiah 61 and says its good news is fulfilled.
    
- Luke 8:1 — Jesus goes through cities “proclaiming and bringing the good news of the Kingdom.”
    

**Meaning in Luke:**  
The “good news” = **Spirit-anointed announcement of release, restoration, and kingdom arrival**.

---

### D. Paul

Paul uses _euangelion_ heavily.  
Examples:

- Romans 1:1–5 — “the gospel of God… concerning His Son… promised beforehand in the Scriptures.”
    
- Romans 1:16 — “the gospel… is the power of God for salvation.”
    

**Meaning for Paul:**  
The “good news” = **God’s saving action in Christ—His death, resurrection, and lordship—promised in the OT**.

## 5. 🧠 Key Nuances to Watch

### A. The Gospel Is News, Not Advice

It is an _announcement_, not a list of spiritual principles.

### B. It Is God’s Action, Not Ours

Mark’s phrase “the kingdom has come near” foregrounds **God’s initiative**.

### C. The Gospel Is Rooted in the OT Story

Jesus and Paul both interpret “gospel” as the fulfillment of OT promises.

### D. The Gospel Is Both Kingdom and Christ-Centered

Jesus: kingdom arrival  
Paul: Christ’s death & resurrection  
Both: aspects of a single story

### E. Always Interpret the Word in Its Context

In Mark 1:15, _euangelion_ = the **announcement of God’s reign**.  
In 1 Corinthians 15:1–4, it = the **announcement of Jesus’ death and resurrection**.

Not contradictory—just **different angles** of the same gospel.

## 7. 📝 Summary for Teaching & Exegesis

When you see _euangelion_ in Week 1 (Mark 1:14–15; Matthew 4:17; Luke 4:16–21):

**The “gospel” = God’s long-promised salvation arriving through Jesus as the fulfillment of Isaiah’s vision of God returning to reign, liberate, restore, and bring His kingdom.**

That’s the core meaning for Jesus’ early ministry.
