# 📘 New Testament Apprenticeship Structure
*A Multi-Phase, Text-Centered Program for Inductive NT Theology*  
**Version 1.0**

---

## 📜 Program Overview

This apprenticeship provides a graduate-level pathway for learning and teaching the New Testament with a focus on:

- Inductive, contextual exegesis  
- Biblical theology arising from the text itself  
- Engaging the Greek New Testament  
- Understanding the Old Testament background behind NT authors  
- Learning how NT writers interpret and apply the OT  
- Developing as a teacher (summaries, outlines, small-group lessons)  
- Avoiding post-biblical systems or creeds as interpretive frameworks  

Assignments include exegesis, Greek analysis, OT connections, teaching summaries, and short essays.

The program is divided into sequential phases, each building on the last.

---

## 🧭 Phase 0 — The Gospel Message in the New Testament (4–6 weeks)

### Purpose
Establish a clear, text-derived understanding of the gospel as proclaimed by:
- Jesus  
- The Apostles  
- The early Church  

This phase lays the theological foundation for all future NT study. You also begin practicing teaching and writing immediately.

### Core Texts
- Mark 1:14–15  
- Matthew 4:17  
- Luke 4:16–21; 24:44–49  
- John 3; John 20:30–31  
- Acts 2; Acts 3; Acts 10  
- Romans 1–5  
- 1 Corinthians 15:1–8  
- Key OT passages referenced in these texts  

### Skills Developed
- Identifying authorial purpose in short passages  
- Seeing the unity and diversity of NT gospel presentations  
- Tracing OT background (MT + LXX)  
- Performing basic Greek word studies  
- Writing clear, concise teaching summaries  

### Assignments
- Exegetical notes  
- Word studies on key Greek terms (*euangelion*, *basileia*, *pistis*, *sōtēr*, etc.)  
- “Gospel synthesis” essays (300–600 words)  
- Teaching summaries  
- Initial entries in the exegetical notebook  

---

## 🧭 Phase 1 — Mark + Philippians (3–4 months)

### Purpose
Build a strong foundation in reading entire NT books inductively and begin Greek reading in earnest.

### Core Texts
- **Mark** (primary introduction to Jesus and the Kingdom)  
- **Philippians** (intro to Pauline argumentation and Christology)

### Skills Developed
- Narrative analysis and identifying pericopes  
- Kingdom of God themes  
- OT allusions in Mark (Isaiah, Psalms, Daniel)  
- Christology of humility and joy in Philippians  
- Greek fundamentals applied to real NT passages  

### Assignments
- Full-book structural observations  
- Pericope-level exegesis  
- Greek parsing exercises  
- Detailed word studies  
- Teaching outlines  
- OT quotation/allusion charts  
- Book overview papers  

---

## 🧭 Phase 2 — Matthew + Acts (4–6 months)

### Purpose
Deepen understanding of Jesus' mission, Israel's Scriptures, and the expansion of the early church.

### Core Texts
- **Matthew** (Jewish Messianic presentation; heavy OT intertextuality)  
- **Acts** (historical and theological account of the early church)

### Skills Developed
- Tracing OT quotations (Masoretic + LXX)  
- Sermon on the Mount exegesis  
- Jewish background of Jesus’ mission  
- Spirit and mission theology in Acts  
- Greek: participles, infinitives, discourse markers  

### Assignments
- OT quotation comparison tables  
- Thematic essays (Kingdom, Messiah, fulfillment)  
- Cultural-historical background essays  
- Teaching summaries for major sections  
- Exegetical projects on apostolic sermons  

---

## 🧭 Phase 3 — Romans, 1 Corinthians, Ephesians (6–9 months)

### Purpose
Engage the heart of Pauline theology using inductive exegesis.

### Core Texts
- **Romans**  
- **1 Corinthians**  
- **Ephesians**

### Skills Developed
- Tracing complex theological arguments  
- Interpreting Abrahamic covenant themes  
- Paul’s understanding of law, Spirit, righteousness, new creation  
- Cultural background of Corinth  
- Greek: clause relationships, logical connectors, argument flow  

### Assignments
- Argument flow charts  
- Pauline theology essays  
- OT background studies  
- Teaching outlines for each major section  
- Online-ready essays or video scripts  

---

## 🧭 Phase 4 — John + 1 John (6 months)

### Purpose
Explore Johannine theology with its high Christology and symbolic depth.

### Core Texts
- **Gospel of John**  
- **1 John**

### Skills Developed
- Johannine symbolism (light, water, bread, shepherd, vine)  
- Temple and Exodus motifs  
- New creation themes  
- Understanding dual meanings in Greek  
- Discourse analysis  

### Assignments
- Symbol tracing projects  
- Christology papers  
- Literary structure diagrams  
- Teaching lessons on major discourses  
- 1 John doctrinal synthesis  

---

## 🧭 Phase 5 — Revelation (3–4 months)

### Purpose
Understand Revelation in its OT-saturated, apocalyptic, pastoral context.

### Core Texts
- **Revelation** (entire book)

### Skills Developed
- Identifying OT allusions  
- Interpreting apocalyptic imagery without speculation  
- First-century political and social background  
- New creation theology  
- Greek: symbolic language, unusual constructions  

### Assignments
- OT allusion charts  
- Vision sequence diagrams  
- Thematic essays (Throne, Lamb, Babylon, New Creation)  
- Teaching summaries of key sections

## 🧩 How to Use This Module in Future Chats

Whenever you start a new chat with your NT Theology Professor GPT:

1. State the **phase** you are in.  
2. State the **week or assignment** you are working on.  
3. Paste any notes or questions for feedback.

**Example:**

> “I am in Phase 0, Week 2, Assignment 1 (Luke 4:16–21).  
> Here are my observations and summary. Please evaluate and refine.”

This module gives the GPT all the context it needs to:
- generate syllabi  
- evaluate assignments  
- create new exercises  
- provide Greek guidance  
- help you advance to the next phase
