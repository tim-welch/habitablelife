# 📚 Exegetical Notebook Template

_For Inductive, Text-Centered New Testament Study_

This template is designed for use across all phases of your NT Apprenticeship.  
Create one folder per NT book, and inside it, create one page per passage or pericope using this structure.

---

## 🏷️ Book:

(Example: _Mark_)

## 📍 Passage:

(Example: _Mark 1:14–15_)

## 📅 Date:

(When you studied this passage)

---

# 1. 📖 Passage Text

Paste or type the passage here (BSB preferred unless textual accuracy requires another translation).  
Keep it visible at the top for continual reference.

---

# 2. 🧩 Literary Context Within the Book

Describe how this passage fits into the flow of the entire book.

Questions to guide you:

- What came before that sets this up?
    
- What comes immediately after?
    
- How does this contribute to the author’s larger message?
    

---

# 3. 📝 Observations (Inductive)

List everything you see in the text.

- Immediate literary context (before/after)
    
- Structure and movement within the passage
    
- Repeated words or key terms
    
- Notable actions, contrasts, or patterns
    
- Conjunctions and logical connectors
    
- Surprises or tensions
    
- Questions that arise naturally
    

Focus on **what the text says**, not interpretation yet.

---

# 4. 🇬🇷 Greek Notes

Include Greek insights that help clarify meaning.

Suggested items:

- Key Greek words (transliteration + basic gloss)
    
- Parsing only when needed (tense/aspect/voice/mood)
    
- Semantic ranges (brief, relevant)
    
- Any syntactical observations
    
- How the Greek contributes to interpretation
    

Optional table format (Markdown-friendly):

|Greek Word|Transliteration|Parsing|Notes / Range|Significance|
|---|---|---|---|---|

---

# 5. 🔗 OT Quotations & Allusions

Record connections that are actually present in the text.

Include:

- Direct quotations (note differences in MT and LXX if important)
    
- Allusions that are textually supported
    
- Original OT context
    
- How the NT author interprets or applies the OT passage
    

---

# 6. 📚 Historical & Cultural Background

Brief and relevant notes only.

- Audience and setting
    
- Socio-political or religious context
    
- Customs referenced
    
- First-century implications for listeners/readers
    

Avoid speculation; anchor in historical evidence.


---

# 7. 🎯 Authorial Purpose (Initial Statement)

Write 2–3 sentences explaining what the inspired author is doing with this passage.  
Focus on _their_ intent, not theological systems or application.

---

# 8. 🧠 Interpretation Summary (3–5 Sentences)

A concise, coherent explanation of what this passage means.

- Use your observations
    
- Use Greek insights as needed
    
- Stay strictly in context
    
- Avoid importing later doctrine
    

This is the heart of your exegetical work.

---

# 9. 🧵 Biblical-Theological Themes

Identify theological truths _that arise from the passage itself_.  
Short bullet points:

- God
    
- Christ
    
- Spirit
    
- Kingdom
    
- Salvation
    
- Covenant themes
    
- New creation
    
- Human response
    

Only include what flows naturally from context.

---

# 10. 🗣 Teaching Summary (Short Paragraph)

Explain the passage as if to a small group or discipleship class in 2–4 minutes:

- Clear
    
- Author-centered
    
- Context-focused
    
- Simple and faithful
    
- No jargon
    

This develops your teaching instincts.

---

# 11. ✍️ Personal Reflection (Optional)

If desired, record:

- Impact on your understanding of Jesus
    
- New insights about the gospel
    
- How this shapes your view of the larger book
    

(Not application-driven—keep it theological or interpretive.)

---

# 12. 📄 Related Notes / Links

Link to:

- Your assignment for this passage
    
- Other passages with similar themes
    
- OT passages referenced
    
- Greek vocabulary notes
    
- Blog drafts related to this text
    

Use Obsidian’s internal linking (`[[Like This]]`) to keep your study interconnected.
