# Philosophy of Theological Triage

## Purpose

This note explains how I prioritize theological beliefs based on their relationship to the gospel, the health of the church, and Christian fellowship. The goal is to hold truth firmly while maintaining unity where possible.

## Definition

**Theological triage** is the process of distinguishing between doctrines of greater and lesser importance. In software engineering, we triage bugs to determine their impact on users and how they rank against other work. The same principle applies to theology: not every disagreement deserves equal attention. Some doctrines are critical because they affect salvation or church faithfulness, while others are less urgent and shouldn’t consume our focus.

## Guiding Principles

1. **Clarity before ranking**  
    Every doctrine must be defined clearly before deciding its importance. Misunderstanding often masquerades as disagreement.
    
2. **The gospel as the standard**  
    The closer a doctrine stands to the heart of the gospel, the more essential it becomes. Any teaching that undermines salvation by grace through faith belongs in the higher tiers.
    
3. **Scriptural weight**  
    Doctrines that are clearly taught and repeatedly emphasized in Scripture carry more authority than those that are mentioned briefly or inferred.
    
4. **Church impact**  
    Some beliefs, though not salvific, shape the health, worship, and order of the local church. These belong to the _primary_ or _secondary_ levels depending on how deeply they affect fellowship.
    
5. **Charity in lesser matters**  
    When Scripture leaves room for multiple faithful interpretations, humility and charity take priority over precision.
    
6. **Consistency with character**  
    Theological precision without love contradicts Christ. “Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up” (1 Cor. 8:1). The goal is faithful discernment that produces unity, not pride.
    

## Diagnostic Questions

When deciding where a doctrine fits:

1. Does denying this teaching compromise the gospel? → **Salvation or Primary**
    
2. Does disagreement over this teaching prevent healthy church life together? → **Secondary**
    
3. Is this mainly a matter of interpretation or conviction that doesn’t affect unity? → **Tertiary**
    
4. Is this teaching central enough that Scripture warns against those who deny it? → **Primary**
    
5. Would mature believers historically have divided over this issue? → **Secondary or Primary**
    

## Practical Application

Theological triage keeps me grounded in conviction without drifting into division.

- It helps me defend essentials with confidence.
    
- It helps me hold secondary issues with conviction and humility.
    
- It helps me treat tertiary issues as opportunities for learning, not fighting.
    

> “In essentials, unity; in non-essentials, liberty; in all things, charity.”  
> — Commonly attributed to **Rupertus Meldenius (Peter Meiderlin)**, 17th-century Lutheran theologian.
