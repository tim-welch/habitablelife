This note explains how to design and maintain doctrine MOCs in a way that balances theological clarity, navigability, and triage consistency.

My [[Philosophy of Theological Triage]] defines the tiers of belief, but doctrine MOCs don’t always need to use all four. The structure should serve the **theology**, not the other way around.

---

## Purpose

Doctrine MOCs organize related beliefs within a specific area of theology.  
Each category shows how its doctrines relate to one another and to the gospel, while linking back to the [[0000 - Statement of Faith]] and the appropriate tier MOC.

A doctrine MOC can take different shapes depending on how broad or focused the topic is.

---

## 1. Single-Tier Doctrine MOCs

**Use when:**  
All doctrines in the category belong to one theological tier.

**Structure:**  
Keep the note organized around the theological or narrative flow of the doctrine rather than by tier headings.

**Examples:**

- [[0020 - The Gospel]] — all beliefs belong to the Salvation tier.
    
- [[Faith and Repentance]] — all beliefs describe the human response to salvation.
    

**Template adjustment:**  
Include a single “Part of” section at the top with the correct tier link, for example:

```markdown
Part of: [[Statement of Faith]] [[Salvation Doctrine]]
```

This keeps backlinks organized without cluttering the content.

---

## 2. Multi-Tier Doctrine MOCs

**Use when:**  
The doctrines within a category span more than one tier.

**Structure:**  
Include headings for each relevant tier (Salvation, Primary, Secondary, Tertiary) and group doctrines accordingly.  
This keeps related beliefs together while still clarifying their relative importance.

**Examples:**

- [[Scripture]] — includes salvation-related truths (the gospel revealed), primary doctrines (authority, inerrancy), and secondary doctrines (interpretation and translation).
    
- [[God and Christ]] — includes salvation (atonement, resurrection), primary (Trinity, deity of Christ), and secondary (providence, image of God).
    

---

## 3. Applied or Thematic Doctrine MOCs

**Use when:**  
The category deals with practical, ethical, or cultural expressions of belief.

**Structure:**  
Focus on lived theology rather than doctrinal hierarchy.  
Tiers may appear, but the logic is usually topical or practical.

**Examples:**

- [[Creation]] — how God’s creative work shapes worldview and worship.
    
- [[Christian Living]] — moral and spiritual application of core truths.
    
- [[Culture and Worship]] — contextual expression of faith.
    

**Tip:**  
It’s fine for these notes to mix tiers if doing so reflects how the topics interact in life or ministry.

---

## Design Priorities

1. **Clarity over uniformity** — each category should make theological sense on its own.
    
2. **Links over lists** — prefer linking to atomic notes and related MOCs rather than expanding long outlines.
    
3. **Context up front** — include a “Part of” section at the top linking to `[[Statement of Faith]]`, `[[Philosophy of Theological Triage]]`, and the relevant tier MOC.
    
4. **Backlinks do the heavy lifting** — rely on backlinks from doctrines to tier MOCs for dynamic organization.
    
5. **Triage as metadata** — use tiers to describe importance, not to dictate note layout.

## Guiding Principle

> Let the theology determine the structure.  
> Use tiers to describe importance, not to define organization.
