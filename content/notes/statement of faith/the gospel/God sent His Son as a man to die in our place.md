## Summary
God became a man in the person of Jesus Christ. He took on human nature so that He could die as our substitute, bearing the penalty we deserved. His death satisfies divine justice and opens the way for reconciliation between God and humanity.

## Biblical Support
- [[John 1#John 1 14|John 1:14]] — The Word became flesh and dwelt among us.  
- [[John 3#John 3 15|John 3:15–16]] — God gave His only Son so that whoever believes in Him may have eternal life.  
- [[Romans 5#Romans 5 8|Romans 5:8]] — God demonstrates His love in that Christ died for us while we were still sinners.  
- [[2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 21|2 Corinthians 5:21]] — He who knew no sin became sin for us.  
- [[1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 24|1 Peter 2:24]] — He bore our sins in His body on the tree.  
- [[Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 14|Hebrews 2:14]] — Through death, He destroyed the one who has the power of death.

## Explanation
Jesus’ incarnation was not symbolic. It was essential. By becoming human, He could die as a true representative for humanity. His voluntary death fulfills the Old Testament pattern of substitutionary sacrifice and demonstrates both God’s justice and mercy.  
The Son’s willingness to enter our condition shows that salvation is entirely an act of divine grace, not human effort.

## Implications
- The incarnation is central to understanding both God’s love and His justice.  
- Without the Son’s full humanity, there could be no true substitution or atonement.  
- This doctrine safeguards the gospel’s core: salvation depends on what Christ has done, not on what we do.  
- It calls believers to humility and gratitude, knowing the cost of redemption.
