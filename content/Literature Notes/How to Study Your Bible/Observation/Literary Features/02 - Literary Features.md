The Bible communicates through literary devices and narrative cues. Recognizing these features keeps us attentive to how meaning is expressed.

- [[Identify genre to guide observation|Connections]] – Genre determines how a passage should be read (poetry vs narrative vs letter).  
- [[Narrative shifts signal transitions|Connections]] – Changes in person, place, time, or event often mark new themes or sections.  
- [[Look for comparisons|Connections]] – Comparisons (like/as) highlight parallels and clarify meaning.  
- [[Look for contrasts|Connections]] – Contrasts sharpen the main point by showing what is and is not meant.  
- [[Look for conclusions|Connections]] – Connective words (therefore, because, so that) signal results or reasoning.  

These features help us notice not only *what* is said but *how* it is communicated.
