### **1. Soteriological Systems (Doctrines of Salvation)**

These deal with divine sovereignty, human will, and salvation.

- **Calvinism** – Stresses God’s absolute sovereignty and predestination (TULIP).
    
- **Arminianism** – Emphasizes conditional election, prevenient grace, and human free response.
    
- **Molinism** – Argues that God knows all possible outcomes (middle knowledge) and actualizes the world where free choices fulfill His will.
    
- **Open Theism** – Holds that God knows all possibilities but the future free choices of creatures are not yet determined.
    
- **Universalism** – Belief that all souls will eventually be reconciled to God.
    
- **Augustinianism** – Precursor to Calvinism; emphasizes original sin and grace as necessary for salvation but with less systematization.
    
- **Pelagianism / Semi-Pelagianism** – Views human nature as able (or partly able) to choose good without divine grace (historically condemned as heresy).
    

---

### **2. Eschatological Systems (End Times)**

These describe how history culminates.

- **Premillennialism** – Christ returns before a literal thousand-year reign.
    
    - _Dispensational Premillennialism_ – Divides history into “dispensations” and distinguishes Israel and the Church.
        
    - _Historic Premillennialism_ – Similar but sees continuity between Israel and the Church.
        
- **Postmillennialism** – The gospel will gradually transform the world before Christ’s return.
    
- **Amillennialism** – The “millennium” is symbolic of Christ’s present reign in heaven and the Church.
    

---

### **3. Ecclesiological Systems (Church and Sacraments)**

How the Church is governed and how sacraments function.

- **Roman Catholicism** – Hierarchical, sacramental, and centered on papal authority and apostolic succession.
    
- **Eastern Orthodoxy** – Emphasizes theosis (deification), liturgy, and continuity with the early Church Fathers.
    
- **Protestantism** – Rooted in _sola scriptura_ and _sola fide_; subdivides into Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican, Baptist, Methodist, Pentecostal, etc.
    
- **Anabaptism** – Focuses on believer’s baptism, nonviolence, and separation from worldly power.
    

---

### **4. Theological Frameworks or Traditions**

Ways Christians systematize or prioritize doctrine.

- **Thomism** – The theological and philosophical synthesis of Thomas Aquinas, integrating Aristotelian logic with Christian theology.
    
- **Wesleyanism** – Methodism’s theology, emphasizing prevenient grace and sanctification.
    
- **Pentecostal/Charismatic Theology** – Focus on the gifts and ongoing work of the Holy Spirit.
    
- **Covenant Theology** – Interprets Scripture through covenants (works, grace, redemption).
    
- **Dispensational Theology** – Sees Scripture as unfolding through distinct ages or dispensations.
    
- **Liberation Theology** – Views salvation in social and political as well as spiritual terms, often emphasizing justice for the oppressed.
    
- **Process Theology** – Influenced by process philosophy; sees God as dynamically interacting with creation.
