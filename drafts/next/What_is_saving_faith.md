# What is saving faith?

From my study of John, I am convinced that, as Paul says in Ephesians, salvation is by grace through faith, not works. This involves:

**Understanding the gospel:** “Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that he was buried, and that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures” (1 Cor 15:3b–4).

**Believing the gospel:** “For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life” (John 3:16).

**Receiving the gospel:** “But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name” (John 1:12).

**Not rejecting the gospel:** “Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life. Whoever rejects the Son will not see life; instead, the wrath of God remains on him” (John 3:36, BSB). The Greek word here (_apeitheō_) can mean “reject,” “disbelieve,” or “disobey,” suggesting that belief is more than agreeing with facts. It is trusting and acting on them.

Belief is like trusting your flight will get you to your destination. You can believe the plane is going there, but if you never board, that belief does you no good.

The practical application is that we must understand, believe, and place our faith in the gospel, which is Jesus’ death for our sins and resurrection. For those who already believe, our calling is to share the gospel clearly and patiently, answering questions until the other person understands. What they choose to do with it is between them and God. Our role is simply to be “prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks… for a reason for the hope that is in you” (1 Pet 3:15).
