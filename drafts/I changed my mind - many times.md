I grew up going to a Congregational church. In high school I took confirmation classes to join the church. During confirmation we had to write an essay about who we think Jesus is. I wasn't sure the pastor of the church actually believed the Bible, so I wrote my essay a "Jesus was team of specially trained angels come to Earth to teach about God" based on the GI Joe cartoon from the 1980s. I knew this wasn't what the Bible said about Jesus but I wanted to see if it would be accepted. It was! But I was reminded that I had to read it in front of the church when I joined, so I rewrote it as a more traditional essay. This is the first time I remember changing my mind: from "we go to church" to "I don't believe." Note that many years later I was told by my aunt that at VBS one year I "accepted Jesus into my heart" but I have no memory of that.

Because the pastor of the church had not firm beliefs, in the late 1980s I decided that Christianity wasn't real or was whatever you wanted it to be. I continued to attend church off and on at various different churches over the years. In the mid to late 1990s I went into my "dark times". Looking back on it, I'm pretty confident I was depressed, but it was never diagnosed. When I look back on that time everything seems dim as if it was in shadow. No change of mind during decade or so. I still didn't believe and moved even farther away from the church.

In the summer of 1999 I was invited to a Bible study talking about the reliability of scripture. I decided to attend. I don't actually remember most of the arguments and data used in this multiweek study, but I remember one of them and got concerned. We were talking about the prophesies Jesus fulfilled. A statistician/mathematician attempted to calculate the chances of a single person fulfilling some of the prophesies. He used population data and various other things I don't understand but believe he did. Based on his calculation, if you covered the entire state of Texas with quarters (it might have been half dollars?) with a single coin marked, then blinded a person and told them to walk around the state and randomly grab one coin, the chances of the person selecting the marked coin was similar to the chances of a single person fulfilling the set of analyzed prophesies. Those were a subset of the prophies Jesus fulfilled. The other thing I remember from that class is that Luke was one of the best historians of his time. As historians and archeologist learn more about the first century AD, they learn that both Luke's Gospel and Acts are very historically accurate. This is the second time I changed my mind. From unbelief to curiosity and agnosticism.

Based on what I learned from that Bible study, I decided I would read Luke's biography of Jesus. Over the month of August I read this book, paying close attention to what Jesus did and said. During that time I came to believe that that God judges sin, expecting us to be absolutely perfect in not only our behavior but our thoughts. I also came to believe Jesus was the son of God who came to offer us forgiveness and a way back to God. I don't know exactly where in book or the month I moved from curiosity to fear to belief to relief, but it happened during August 1999. This is a third change of mind from agnosticism to belief.
