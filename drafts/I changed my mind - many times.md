I grew up going to a Congregational church. In high school I took confirmation classes to join the church. During confirmation we had to write an essay about who we think Jesus is. I wasn't sure the pastor of the church actually believed the Bible, so I wrote my essay a "Jesus was team of specially trained angels come to Earth to teach about God" based on the GI Joe cartoon from the 1980s. I knew this wasn't what the Bible said about Jesus but I wanted to see if it would be accepted. It was! But I was reminded that I had to read it in front of the church when I joined, so I rewrote it as a more traditional essay. This is the first time I remember changing my mind: from "we go to church" to "I don't believe." Note that many years later I was told by my aunt that at VBS one year I "accepted Jesus into my heart" but I have no memory of that.

Because the pastor of the church had not firm beliefs, in the late 1980s I decided that Christianity wasn't real or was whatever you wanted it to be. I continued to attend church off and on at various different churches over the years. In the mid to late 1990s I went into my "dark times". Looking back on it, I'm pretty confident I was depressed, but it was never diagnosed. When I look back on that time everything seems dim as if it was in shadow. No change of mind during decade or so. I still didn't believe and moved even farther away from the church.

In the summer of 1999 I was invited to a Bible study talking about the reliability of scripture. I decided to attend. I don't actually remember most of the arguments and data used in this multiweek study, but I remember one of them and got concerned. We were talking about the prophesies Jesus fulfilled. A statistician/mathematician attempted to calculate the chances of a single person fulfilling some of the prophesies. He used population data and various other things I don't understand but believe he did. Based on his calculation, if you covered the entire state of Texas with quarters (it might have been half dollars?) with a single coin marked, then blinded a person and told them to walk around the state and randomly grab one coin, the chances of the person selecting the marked coin was similar to the chances of a single person fulfilling the set of analyzed prophesies. Those were a subset of the prophies Jesus fulfilled. The other thing I remember from that class is that Luke was one of the best historians of his time. As historians and archeologist learn more about the first century AD, they learn that both Luke's Gospel and Acts are very historically accurate. This is the second time I changed my mind. From unbelief to curiosity and agnosticism.

Based on what I learned from that Bible study, I decided I would read Luke's biography of Jesus. Over the month of August I read this book, paying close attention to what Jesus did and said. During that time I came to believe that that God judges sin, expecting us to be absolutely perfect in not only our behavior but our thoughts. I also came to believe Jesus was the son of God who came to offer us forgiveness and a way back to God. I don't know exactly where in book or the month I moved from curiosity to fear to belief to relief, but it happened during August 1999. This is a third change of mind from agnosticism to belief.

As a new believer I started attending the church that hosted the Bible study and started learning. I read the entire NASB. Soon I was told about a new church that was much smaller where I might be able to work with the pastor and learn more about the faith. So I started attending. I met regularly with the pastor and growing in my understanding. Then he got sick of New England winters and moved to Florida. The church changed and we had a couple of people preaching before deciding on one. Around this time I read the entire NIV in chronological order. I continued to grow in my faith an understanding.

Then Doug also joined our church. He started teaching a Bible study. He came from Grace Community Church where he sat under John McArthur. He taught a Bible study called Foundations of the Faith. Around this time I also started listening to R.C. Sproul and John Piper. I read books by all three men as well as continued my study with Doug. Without knowing it, I became a 5-point Calvinist who believed in God's Sovereignty as God being in meticulous control of everything. "There isn't a rouge molecule in the universe." I explained God's sovereignty and goodness by thinking of God as an author. C.S. Lewis isn't evil even though he wrote "The Lion, the Witch, and the Wardrobe" and made the witch do evil things. Lewis was in meticulous control of everything that happened in the book, but it was the witch who deserved to be punished for her bad deeds. Lewis was not morally responsible for them. My forth change of mind was from simple believe that Jesus was the Son of God and paid for my sin so I could be forgiven and gain eternal life to full-blown Calvinism.

For around 2 decades I studied Calvinism growing in my understanding. I continued to read the Bible, but over time it became less and less. Most of my time was spent listening to Calvinist preachers, I added Paul Washer to the list, reading books by Calvinists, and Calvinist Bible studies. I changed churches a few more times, eventually landing in one that was openly Reformed (another name for Calvinism). I continued to learn under the paster of this church and continued to grow in my understanding.

Then something changed. Somewhere around 2023 my wife got more serious about her Bible study and started asking a lot of questions. I decided that rather than just answering based on the theology I used I should go back to my study of the Bible like I used to do. That was a combination of reading through the Bible and reading individual books repeatedly until I thought I understood the author's argument. I started with James, then Galatians, then John. During those studies I started seeing things that seemed to contradict what I was learning. The first was my understanding of God's Sovereignty. Remember I had become convinced that God was telling a story to bring Himself maximum glory. Everything we believed, did, and said was under God's meticulous control. Therefore, to believe in God, God had to give me faith. But it was more. People sinned because God determined that they would sin. They were responsible and deserving of punishment, but God was the ultimate cause, the first cause, of everything including sin. But as I read James, it really seemed like James was writing to people who could make choices and when they weren't confident in what they should do they could ask God for wisdom and He would give them the wisdom they needed. But it was their choice how they would respond to the trials they were going through. I noticed that I was using respond and and response to trials and that this word is closely related to responsible and responsibility. This got me thinking everyone is responsible for their response to given situations and God was going to punish them for their sinful responses. But if people only able to respond in the way that God determined them to respond then how are they responsible for that response? If God is truly Good and Just, and God judges us for our sins, then there must be some amount of choice we have to be able to make. My view of sovereignty changed. I looked up the definition and started thinking about what sovereignty would mean when used in normally. A sovereign king has authority to make rules and judge but not that they would control every event that happened in their kingdom. They set the rules. They expected the rules to be followed. They delegated authority and responsibility to others all the way down to the lowest serf and slave. That isn't what I was taught about God's Sovereignty (it was always capitalized in my mind). It occurred to me that the Biblical authors might be using "sovereignty" the way a normal person would. This was my next change of mind from God predetermining absolutely everything that happens in every detail before the beginning of time to us having some real ability to respond that we are responsible for. 

I continued to study, moving from James to Galatians and The Gospel John. In both of these I saw belief being the key to unlocking salvation, forgiveness, and eternal life. And it appeared that Jesus was offering eternal life to all who belief. This was the first chink in the 5-points of Calvinism. Was Jesus' sacrifice for everyone or was it limited. As I studied more it became clear that Jesus was at the very least offering salvation to everyone. He died for the whole world; he died for everyone. This was my next change of mind limited atonement was wrong Jesus died for everyone.

Then came irresistible grace. Jesus clearly taught that He wanted everyone to be saved but they refused. They rejected Him because they loved their sin. Everyone who believed was given eternal life, but the wrath of God remains on those who reject Him. My next change of mind was to reject irresistible grace; people have the ability to reject Jesus and not believe.

If irresistible grace is wrong, and people have a choice to believe or reject, then total depravity (meaning an inability to believe) and unconditional election can't stand. The only way for us to be able to reject is for us to be able to accept. If we have no ability to accept Jesus' teaching and believe in Him, then all we are able to do is reject and disbelieve. We would have no ability to respond in either way if we don't have the ability to respond in both ways. So my next change of mind was to drop total depravity.

After losing sovereignty, total depravity, unconditional election, limited atonement, and irresistible grace, the only thing remaining from Calvinism is perseverance of the saints. I honestly haven't landed on that one yet. But the Calvinistic teaching that once God has irresistibly changed an elected person's heart to save them and give them eternal life then they will persevere with good works is not what I believe. Where I am not sure is whether once a person believes and is give eternal life is it possible for them to stop believing and lose that life. I am still studying this and have not made up my mind here.

So, what do I believe?

1. I have sinned and have not lived up to the standard God expects.
2. What we deserve for sinning is death.
3. Nothing we do can pay the debt we owe. If we try, our best work is nothing but dirty rags in God's sight.
4. God sent His Son as a man to die in our place.
5. Jesus' death was atonement for all sin.
6. Whoever believes in Jesus has eternal life.
7. Whoever rejects Jesus will not see life, instead the wrath of God remains on them.

What do we have to believe about Jesus?

1. Jesus died and was buried, paying the penalty of sin.
2. Jesus rose again on the third day, proving that the payment was accepted.
3. There is no other name by which we can be saved.
4. There is nothing we can do to add to the work Jesus has done. We believe and and are saved.
