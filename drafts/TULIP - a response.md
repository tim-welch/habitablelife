# TULIP

The 5 Points of Calvinism, as described [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Points_of_Calvinism), summarize the Reformed view of salvation.

## Total depravity
Total depravity, derived from Calvin's interpretation of Augustine's view on original sin, asserts that due to the fall, every person is enslaved to sin and morally unable to choose salvation, with sin affecting every part of a person, though not implying complete evil.

### Response to total depravity
Many aspects of this doctrine are biblical. For example: "Every person is enslaved to sin," as Jesus taught: "Everyone who sins is a slave to sin" (John 8:34). "People are not by nature inclined to love God but rather to serve their own interests and reject God's rule," as Jesus said: "Men loved darkness rather than the Light because their deeds were evil" (John 3:19). "The inability to save oneself from sin" is also taught by Jesus: "No one can come to Me unless the Father who sent Me draws him" (John 6:44). No Bible believer can reject these teachings, but does this lead to all people being "morally unable to choose to trust God for their salvation"? Sort of. We cannot on our own gain eternal life. Fortunately, God did not leave us on our own. In His sovereignty and for His glory, God decided in eternity past to intervene by sending Christ — God incarnate — to be crucified for our sins and rise again in eternal life. Through Christ’s crucifixion, God draws everyone to Himself (John 12:32). It is only by God’s power, through the reading of the Word and the preaching of believers, that unbelievers are given the opportunity to believe and receive eternal life.

## Unconditional election
Unconditional election asserts that God has chosen, from eternity, who will receive salvation based solely on His mercy, not on any merit or faith in the individual, with the chosen receiving salvation through Christ and the non-chosen receiving just punishment for their sins.

### Response to unconditional election
While those who are saved are not saved based on foreseen virtue or merit, Jesus Himself said, "For God so loved the world that He gave His one and only Son, that everyone who believes in Him shall not perish but have eternal life" (John 3:16). He also said, "Whoever believes in Him is not condemned, but whoever does not believe has already been condemned, because he has not believed in the name of God’s one and only Son" (John 3:18), and "Whoever hears My word and believes Him who sent Me has eternal life and will not come under judgment" (John 5:24). Jesus even said, "I say these things so that you may be saved" (John 5:34). John tells us, "To all who did receive Him, to those who believed in His name, He gave the right to become children of God" (John 1:12). Given that Jesus and John taught that belief is the criteria for eternal life, it is hard to accept that a person’s faith is not what God bases salvation on.

## Limited atonement
Limited atonement teaches that Jesus's death atoned only for the sins of the elect, with its value and power being unlimited, but its purpose specifically intended for those chosen by God.

### Response to limited atonement
This teaching seems to contradict the plain reading of the Bible. John the Baptist describes Jesus as "the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world." John, speaking about Jesus, says, "He Himself is the atoning sacrifice for our sins, and not only for ours but also for the sins of the whole world" (1 John 2:2), and "that through Him everyone might believe" (John 1:7). Paul tells Timothy that Jesus "gave Himself as a ransom for all" (1 Timothy 2:6) and "is the Savior of everyone, and especially of those who believe" (1 Timothy 4:10).

## Irresistible grace
Irresistible grace asserts that God's saving grace is effectively applied to the elect, overcoming their resistance and ensuring their salvation, as the Holy Spirit causes them to cooperate with faith, repentance, and coming to Christ, despite the outward call often being rejected.

### Response to irresistible grace
The Bible teaches throughout the New Testament, and especially in the Gospel according to John, that those who believe in Jesus Christ, the Son of God, are not condemned but have eternal life. Despite this good news, the proclamation of the gospel is often rejected. Therefore, it must be true that God’s grace is resistible.

## Perseverance of the saints
Perseverance of the saints asserts that, due to God's sovereignty, those predestined to salvation will remain in faith until the end, with those who fall away either never having had true faith or being chastened by God to repent and return to Him.

### Response to perseverance of the saints
The Wikipedia article uses some good passages to explain that those who have true faith will remain in the faith despite not living perfectly. In these two passages mentioned in the article we are given examples of people who claimed belief but then proved their believe was not real. John tells his readers in 1 John that many antichrists left Christianity proving they never belonged (1 John 2:18-19). And the writer to the Hebrews explains that God will discipline and rebuke His children for their own good. If they don't experience discipline then they are not truly God's children (Hebrews 12:5-11). While these passages demonstrate that there are people who profess faith but don't really believe, they do not talk about whether or not God's sovereignty can be frustrated by humans.
